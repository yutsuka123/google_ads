Science Lab — physics you can operate

An educational site that lets you move, change and measure natural phenomena in the browser instead of only reading about them. It starts with electromagnetism: from what an electric current really is, through Maxwell's equations, to antennas, lightning and semiconductors.

  • What electric current really is Electrons do not simply rush along a wire: an electric field spreads, and the electrons start to move. Compare the speed of light with the speed of the electrons.
  • Fields spreading as circles (2D and 3D) Maxwell's equations solved numerically in a cross-section of a wire: when current starts to flow, electric and magnetic fields spread out as circles. A 3D view lets you change the viewpoint and zoom.
  • Electric field lines and equipotentials Around two point charges the field lines and equipotential lines always cross at right angles. Change the charge ratio and watch the "potential landscape".
  • Vector calculus (∇, gradient, divergence, curl) A review of derivatives and integrals, then ∇, gradient, divergence, curl, the Laplacian, the divergence and Stokes theorems, and cylindrical and spherical coordinates — with figures and step-by-step equations. The gradient and curl chapters have 3D figures you can move with the mouse or a finger.
  • Maxwell's equations Gauss's laws for electric and magnetic fields, Faraday's law and the Ampère–Maxwell law (displacement current), with figures, step-by-step equations and numbers. From the four equations we derive the electromagnetic wave equation, the speed of light c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀), and energy conservation (Poynting's theorem).
  • Antennas How electrons oscillating in a conductor become radio waves, and how antenna length changes the radiation pattern and radiation resistance.
  • Lightning and lightning rods Why the electric field concentrates at a thin tip, and whether it reaches the breakdown threshold of air, computed from an exact solution.
  • Inside a semiconductor Electrons and holes in a PN junction, a diode and a transistor, from a one-dimensional drift–diffusion model solved numerically and checked against analytic results.
  • Circuit editor Place batteries, AC sources, resistors, capacitors, coils, transformers, diodes and transistors, then see current, voltage, fields and electron motion at the same time.

Science Lab is an early-access (alpha) release. Its pages are labelled as not yet reviewed, and we correct them as we check. The pages are currently written in Japanese.
